YouTube Music, forged for offline.

A YouTube Music player for Android. Downloads run at full speed, playback works with no signal, and Home is built from what you actually play rather than what a server guessed. Built and tested on an Honor X7b because I was tired of apps that treat a budget phone like a crime scene.

Why this exists

I wanted three things.

I wanted something that downloads fast, works offline, and doesn’t fill Home with stuff I never asked for. So this got built.

  1. 01

    Downloads shouldn’t crawl.

    Google throttles any download bigger than about 10 MB to roughly 30 kB/s. Dial-up with extra steps. MusicForge asks for 4 MB at a time, so a long mix pulls at 3 to 5 MB/s on home Wi-Fi. It still takes long enough to make you question your life choices.

  2. 02

    Home should be about you.

    Other apps fill your Home with stuff you never asked for. Ours is built from what you actually play — recently played, around this hour, on repeat, liked and forgotten. Every shelf says why it’s there. Long-press anything for “not for me.” You will.

  3. 03

    Offline means offline.

    Downloaded songs start in about 0.3 seconds with no signal at all. Search still finds your own music. Downloads survive the app being killed, which on Honor and Xiaomi phones happens for sport.

What it does

Six things it does well.

  • A Home that’s yours

    Recently played, around this hour, on repeat, liked then forgotten. Every shelf says why it’s there, and items carry where they came from, including the YouTube feed, which is labelled as such.

  • Fast downloads

    Bounded 4 MB ranges, up to three downloads at once, and a rule that a mid-file re-resolve must return the same file length, so it fails rather than splicing two different renditions together.

  • Sign-in sync

    Sign in with Google and your liked songs, playlists, albums and artists come across and stay in sync. Search runs as you too, unless you turn that off in settings.

  • Offline first

    The player reads your downloads before it touches the network. Downloads and playback survive the process dying, and at startup the app repairs its database from the download index.

  • Liquid glass

    The blurred backdrop sits on the nav pill and the mini player, and nowhere else. A Snapdragon 680 has a frame budget to keep, so the backdrop is recorded once and sampled by those two surfaces. It never sits behind a scrolling list.

  • Export to Music

    Copies finished downloads to Music/MusicForge as normal tagged Ogg files, with the cover and lyrics written in. Another player, your PC, or a USB stick can all read them.

Things that broke

Everything here cost a day.

One phone, one developer, a lot of logs. These are the ones worth reading about.

YouTube returns 400 for languages it doesn’t know.

My phone runs en-ZA. YouTube doesn’t recognize it and sends 400 on every request — no home, no search, no playback, no downloads. The emulator runs en-US, so everything looked fine there. I caused this by testing on the wrong phone and not noticing until I held the real one. Now I only send language tags YouTube actually knows.

Requests over ~10 MB crawl.

One request on a 70 MB stream does 0.03 MB/s. Split into 4 MB ranges, the same stream does 5 to 6 MB/s. I assumed one big request was a good idea. It wasn’t.

Rotted picture links.

About 3% of artist and playlist pictures in a restored library just 404 now. The links died upstream. I didn’t rot the internet, but I made you stare at the gaps. The app re-looks them up when they fail.

Overdraw.

Every cover on Home was painted four times per frame. On an Adreno 610 that cost more than any animation ever did. I baked the scrim once per track and stripped the window background. My phone’s GPU still hasn’t forgiven me.

FAQ

The usual questions.

Do I need a Google account?

No. It browses and plays signed out. Signing in brings your liked songs, playlists, albums and artists across and keeps them in sync. You can pause search history if you’d rather search anonymously. I don’t need your account and I don’t want it.

Which phones does it run on?

Android 8.0 and up. Built and tested on an Honor X7b, so anything with more headroom than a Snapdragon 680 should be fine. On Honor, Huawei and Xiaomi, let it run in the background when it asks, or the system kills playback.

Can I bring my library from Metrolist or ConvX?

Yes. Point it at one of their backups and it restores the library, sign-in included, then offers to re-download the songs you had. Settings come across by their original key names. I borrowed the backup format; the bugs are mine.

Where do downloads go?

In the app’s own offline storage, where the player finds them instantly. Export copies them to Music/MusicForge as tagged Ogg files with covers and lyrics. WebM gets remuxed because MediaStore refuses audio/webm in Music. Blame Google, not me.

Is it on the Play Store?

No. The APK is on GitHub Releases, and it installs beside whatever you use now. Sideloading is a setting away on most phones. Google won’t host it and I won’t ask them.

Why does the launcher say “Sonata”?

Each phase gets a codename until the app has a real name. This one is Sonata, 0.14. The package ID has stayed the same the whole time, so your data survives the renames. I couldn’t decide on a name.

What isn’t done yet?

Crossfade, multi-select, and the player still drops a frame or two when it opens. Those are written down rather than quietly forgotten. Don’t hold your breath.

What does it cost?

Nothing. GPL-3.0. Source on GitHub. No ads, no account to register, no one to sell your data to. You get what you pay for.
