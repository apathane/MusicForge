01 / THE IDEA

A music app should still be a music app when the internet leaves.

MusicForge started with a simple frustration: downloads that crawl, libraries that feel rented, and home screens that keep asking for attention instead of helping you choose a song.

So the project is built around the boring-but-important parts: a download that resumes, a local library that remains useful, and an interface that gets out of the way when the music starts.

03 / UNDER THE HOOD

Fast isn't a vibe.
It's a design decision.

MusicForge uses bounded range requests because one enormous audio request can be throttled. Small, resumable chunks are a better fit for long tracks and ordinary connections.

04 / THE SURFACE

Quiet canvas.
Expressive details.

The interface starts monochrome, then lets the music bring the color. Two surface treatments are designed to be choices, not an excuse to blur every rectangle in sight.

Frosted Crayon

Artwork-tinted frost, soft edges, and a warmer, more illustrated feel.

Liquid Glass

Live backdrop blur, a refractive edge, specular highlights, and a clearer sense of depth on supported devices.

Liquid Glass is deliberately limited to small floating controls, not full-screen scrolling content. Performance gets a vote.

05 / FIELD NOTES

Built on a real phone.
Measured, then tuned.

The test target is an Honor X7b with a Snapdragon 680. That means render cost, memory, and download behaviour are constraints, not afterthoughts.

TRANSFER STRATEGY
4 MiB

Default range size. Small enough to stay below the observed throttling threshold, large enough to avoid needless round trips.

STEADY SEARCH SCROLL
1.8%

Janky frames across 169 frames in the recorded emulator test. Not a universal device guarantee.

LONG HOME FLINGS
8.3%

Recorded janky frames while shelves and continuation pages entered during long flings. This is the work still worth doing.

↳ Honest measurements include the rough edges. See the full test setup, trade-offs, and next checks in the repository.
