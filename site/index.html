Why this exists

I wanted three things.

I wanted something that downloads fast, works offline, and doesn’t fill Home with stuff I never asked for. So this got built.

  1. 01

    Downloads shouldn’t crawl.

    Google throttles any download bigger than about 10 MB to roughly 30 kB/s. MusicForge asks for 4 MB at a time, so a long mix comes in at 3 to 5 MB/s on home Wi-Fi instead of at playback speed.

  2. 02

    Home should be about you.

    Shelves are composed from what you played last, what you play around this hour, what’s on repeat and what you liked and forgot. Each one says why it’s there. Long-press anything for “not for me”.

  3. 03

    Offline means offline.

    Downloaded songs start in about 0.3 s with no signal at all, and search still finds your own music. Downloads survive the app being killed, which on some phones happens for sport.

What it does

Six things it does well.

  • A Home that’s yours

    Recently played, around this hour, on repeat, liked then forgotten. Every shelf says why it’s there, and items carry where they came from, including the YouTube feed, which is labelled as such.

  • Fast downloads

    Bounded 4 MB ranges, up to three downloads at once, and a rule that a mid-file re-resolve must return the same file length, so it fails rather than splicing two different renditions together.

  • Sign-in sync

    Sign in with Google and your liked songs, playlists, albums and artists come across and stay in sync. Search runs as you too, unless you turn that off in settings.

  • Offline first

    The player reads your downloads before it touches the network. Downloads and playback survive the process dying, and at startup the app repairs its database from the download index.

  • Liquid glass

    The blurred backdrop sits on the nav pill and the mini player, and nowhere else. A Snapdragon 680 has a frame budget to keep, so the backdrop is recorded once and sampled by those two surfaces. It never sits behind a scrolling list.

  • Export to Music

    Copies finished downloads to Music/MusicForge as normal tagged Ogg files, with the cover and lyrics written in. Another player, your PC, or a USB stick can all read them.

Things that broke

Everything here cost a day.

One phone, one developer, a lot of logs. These are the ones worth reading about.

YouTube returns 400 for languages it doesn’t know.

My phone is set to en-ZA. On the emulator, which is en-US, everything worked. On the phone, nothing did: no home, no search, no playback, no downloads. Only the hl values on YouTube’s own list get sent now.

Requests over ~10 MB crawl.

A 70 MB stream downloads at 0.03 MB/s in one request. That is dial-up with extra steps. Split into 4 MB ranges, the same stream does 5 to 6 MB/s. The measurements are in docs/BENCHMARKS.md.

Old libraries have dead pictures.

About 3% of the artist and playlist pictures in a restored library just 404 now, because the links rotted upstream. The app looks the picture up again when it fails and saves the new link.

Every cover on Home was painted four times a frame.

That’s overdraw, and on an Adreno 610 it cost more than any animation ever did. The window background goes after setContent, artwork is decoded at slot size, and the player’s scrim is baked once per track.

FAQ

The usual questions.

Do I need a Google account?

No. It browses and plays signed out. Signing in is what brings your liked songs, playlists, albums and artists across and keeps them in sync; you can pause search history separately if you’d rather search anonymously.

Which phones does it run on?

Android 8.0 and up. It’s built and tested on an Honor X7b, so anything with more headroom than a Snapdragon 680 should be fine. On Honor, Huawei and Xiaomi phones, let it run in the background when it asks, or the system will kill playback.

Can I bring my library from Metrolist or ConvX?

Yes. Point it at one of their backups and it restores the library, sign-in included, then offers to re-download the songs you had. Settings come across by their original key names, which is why your preferences survive too.

Where do downloads go?

Downloads live in the app’s own offline storage, where the player can find them instantly. Export copies them to Music/MusicForge on shared storage as tagged Ogg files with covers and lyrics, so other apps can see them. WebM gets remuxed, because MediaStore refuses audio/webm in Music.

Is it on the Play Store?

No. The APK is on GitHub Releases, and it installs under its own package name, next to whatever you use now. Sideloading is a setting away on most phones.

Why does the launcher icon say “Sonata”?

Each development phase gets a codename, and the launcher label is the codename until the app gets a real name. This one is Sonata, 0.14. The package id has stayed dev.musicforge.app the whole time, so your data survives the renames.

What isn’t done yet?

Crossfade, multi-select, and the player still drops a frame or two when it opens. Those are written down rather than quietly forgotten.

What does it cost?

Nothing. It’s GPL-3.0 and the source is on GitHub. There are no ads, and nobody’s account to register for.
